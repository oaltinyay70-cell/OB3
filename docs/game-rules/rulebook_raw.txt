1. Introduction 
In Obscure Battles Volume 3 – Drone Commander, the player takes the role of a Drone Commander trying to locate and destroy enemy 
assets using one of the Drone Types. 
Drone strikes have played a key role in recent conflicts the recent examples such as bolstering the Addis Ababa government's position in 
the face of attacks from TPLF rebels (The Ethiopian Government has purchased armed drones from Turkey). The UAE similarly supplied 
Chinese-built drones to its ally General Khalifa Haftar in Libya's brutal civil war. In many cases armed drones have had a decisive impact, 
contributing to the survival of Libya's internationally recognized government in Tripoli and, in last year's Nagorno-Karabakh conflict, TB2s 
were a potent factor enabling Azerbaijan's forces to wrest control of the disputed enclave from Armenia. 
While researching for the game, the game designers also came to realize that the "unmanned" aspect of armed UAVs has raised moral 
concerns about their use in combat and law enforcement contexts. Attacking humans with remote-controlled machines is even more 
abstract than the use of other "stand-off" weaponry, such as missiles, artillery, and aerial bombardment, depersonalizing the decision to 
attack. By contrast, UAVs and other stand-off systems reduce casualties among the attackers. 
In this perspective, this game may feature scenarios that parallel the reality in the world, but they may look “evil” and/or “politically 
incorrect” to some however it should be understood that such missions actually already happened. Please keep this in the back of your 
mind. 
2. How the Game is Played 
This game is a board game that is a simulation of a Drone Operation that takes on the enemy based on its own sensory input and in some 
scenarios on scripted conditional engagements. As part of the game, there are different Drone types operated by you taking on the enemy 
that is decided by the scenario settings. 
Unlike the other Obscure Battle Series this game is solitaire: you do not need any other player to play the game. All you have to have are a 
piece paper on which the game is printed , and one 1 x 6 and one 1 X 10 -sided dice to play the game. 
There is however a 2+ -player mode where players run the same scenario and compete against each other to complete the requirements 
of the scenario faster than the competing scenario. 
The game will make use of several rules, charts, and associated tables to execute the battlefield simulation as its play system mechanics. 
2.1. The Game Play 
The game consists of turns. 
One turn consists of your drone starting from Box 0, traversing all Boxes up to and including B6 and leaving B6 Box safe and sound back to 
B0 – to start the next turn.  
The Drone Manager ™ procedure in the game will help you to simulate the “find, fix, finish” process of a drone commander. 
There are 2 modes of play – solitaire and 2 player+ version where players compete against each other.

2.9.3. Drone System Health box 
This is where the fundamentals of the drone you are using is recorded. 
It has tracking table for Damage (Structural Integrity, Comms and Sensors), fuel situation, Altitude Marker, Weapon stations load out. 
All Indicators other than FUEL start at 0. FUEL ONBOARD starts at the fuel rating value of the drone used. 
 
INTEGRITY DAMAGE is the damage to the structural integrity of the drone you are flying. Your drone may take some damage and keep 
flying but losing integrity will also give way to additional damages to other components and may increase your visibility VIS to the enemy 
sensors making an easier target. 
SENSORS DAMAGE monitors the damage to the sensors of the Drone. Increased damage to Sensors will lead to a mission kill of your drone 
since your drone will not be able to effectively locate and attacks the targets. 
VISIBILITY VIS/RCS is the visibility of your drone in Radar/IR and plain eyeball MK1 sensory equipment. More visible a drone becomes , it 
will be an easier target since its IR signature and RCS ( Radar Cross Section) will be larger. 
ONBOARD FUEL is what your drone carries as fuel in its belly. 
COMMS is the ability of your drone getting commands from the ground station. If you lose the COMMS link, you won’t be able to 
command your drone it will be lost. COMMs damages have a limiting value of 5.

2.10. 
Combat Cards 
Combat Cards are used widely in other Obscure Battles games and designed to give extra randomness of the battlefield to the gameplay. 
Additional scenarios may carry additional cards. 
Some cards have events or conditions that need to be fulfilled or applied to your Drone. These take precedence over the normal rules. 
They reflect unexpected developments that happen in the real life and add to the variability of the gameplay. 
Some Combat Cards are “No Event” Cards – when the player draws one of these, s/he does not need to do anything but simply discard the 
card into the discard pile. 
If Combat cards are all used up during a game, then re-shuffle the cards and put them back onto the board face-up. 
Before the start of the game, the cards are shuffled into one stack and placed on the table and used when the players visit a box. 
3. Game Terms & Abbreviations & Format 
Some of the abbreviations used in the game are as follows: 
CRT: Combat Resolution Table 
DISCARDED THREAT AND COMBAT CARDS PILE: Any card that is revealed and then used is kept here until the end of the game, only 
destroyed “Target Cards” are not here. 
DESTROYED TARGET CARD PILE: Any Target that is destroyed must have its “Target Card” moved here after the attack has taken place. 
DR: Dice Roll, in this game it is a 1D6 meaning 1 roll of a six-sided dice. DR cannot be more than six or less than 1. 
DRM: Dice Roll Modified, the final roll number with any increase or decrease to the initial rolled number because of the game factors. 
There are two types of Dice used in the game and max DRM can be higher than 10 or lower than 1 thanks to the modifiers.

5.2. 2+ Player Mode 
It is possible that the game  can be played in a 2+ player mode , with players competing against each other on the gameboard suing the 
same solitaire quick game rules, playing against the same deck of threats , target and combat cards ( both sides select from the same 
decks for all ). 
In such games the winner is decided as follows: 
1. 
First player to reach mission objectives wins. 
2. 
If both players lose their drones before reaching the goals of the scenario, the player who has amassed the higher number of 
VPs wins. 
5.3. Scenario Games 
Scenario games are 1 player games where the player is given strict orders to be followed to reach the scenario mission goal(s). Some 
scenario may use their own Target and Threat probability distribution ranges but unless otherwise stated you must follow the base range 
set used in this rule book. 
5.4. Campaign Game 
Campaign games introduce the Strategic vision where you have to decide how and where to conduct your next mission. 
In a campaign game you start at Square 1. This is where your current base is. You can only conduct missions in the zones that are adjacent 
to this zone. 
Each zone in the map has a list of specific threats and targets. Once you eliminate all the targets in a zone, it becomes your territory and 
you can start conducting missions from that zone. 
Your campaign objectives are stated in the campaign document file and generally about either taking all enemy territory or eliminating a 
certain target for which you have to take at least some of the territory boxes. 
When you start a Campaign, you may have a limited number and type of Drones but it is possible in some scenarios to receive 
reinforcement in forms of newer and better equipped drones. These are documented in the campaign documents. 
Campaigns may also have different probability ranges for Target and Threat types, these take precedence over the base ranges since theyu 
reflect “the reality” of the campaign conditions.

6.4.5. Taking Damage 
Your drone is likely to receive damage in the form of (D) points int the counter fire from the threat after it has attacked a target.  
This damage realistically affects all the subcomponents of the Drone you are using. This means that when you receive damage to the 
Drone Body it will affect your other subcomponents such as “COMMS” (Communications) and “SENSORS” (Sensors). Additionally, while 
not a damage, damage will also increase your Visibility level (VIS/RCS), making your drone an easier target for the enemy. 
While most threat that you will encounter will inflict mostly damage to your drone’s Structural Integrity, some of them may deliver 
different types of damages. 
The damage received during the mission is distributed according to the following procedure: 
6.4.5.1. Taking Structural Integrity Damage 
Every 1D damage result received during the combat is registered directly to the “Structural Integrity” Damage Column on the 
Drone Info Card. 
 Each type of drone has a specific Structural Integrity strength, if your drone receives damage that is equal to this number, then 
it is assumed destroyed. 
6.4.5.2. Taking Sensors Damage 
 
For Every 2 points of damage registered on the “Structural Integrity” Damage Column, raise the Damage Level on the 
“SENSORS” Damage column by 1. 
 
Maximum Sensor Damage is 9 regardless of the structural Integrity Damage to your Drone. 
For every 4 points of Sensor Damage to your drone add -1 to your Drone Attack Combat Roll. 
Optional Sensor Kits do not take damage as they are not part of the body of the Drone but external kits.. 
6.4.5.3. Taking COMMs Damage 
 
For Every 3 points of  damage registered on the “Structural Integrity” Damage Column, raise the Damage Level on the 
“COMMS” Damage column by 1 
 
This check is done for each turn and effects are only for 1 turn unless Drone becomes uncontrollable in which case the game 
ends. 
 
After your Drone receives more than 2 points of COMMS damage, every turn it is placed on the B0 box it has to take a COMMS 
check roll to see if it is still can be controlled. If it fails the check, it is assumed that the contact has been lost to your drone and 
it is subsequently destroyed and game ends.

7. Scoring 
There are a few scoring conventions for the Drone Commander game. 
Maximum Kill Method 
Once the game has ends once the Target card deck has been totally expanded, take all the cards from the “Destroyed Target Card” pile 
and sum up the VP points printed on the upper right corner. This is your final score for the “Maximum Kill” scoring mode. 
Quick Kill 
The game ends immediately when you finish your main objective regardless of the survival of your drone. 
Take all the cards from the “Destroyed Target Card” pile and sum up the VP points printed on the upper right corner. This is your final 
score for the “Quick Kill” scoring mode.
